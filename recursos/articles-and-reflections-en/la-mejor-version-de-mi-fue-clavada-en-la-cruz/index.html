The best version of me was nailed to the cross

Many preachers today talk about becoming “the best version of ourselves.” They speak about developing our potential, strengthening our character, overcoming our limitations, and becoming better men, better women, better Christians.

And probably many of us disagree with that teaching. But here is the problem: even though we may not believe it, many times we live exactly that way. We try to become the best version of ourselves through our own strength. We try to control our character, control our thoughts, be more patient, sin less, love more, pray more, behave better. And without realizing it, we are still trusting in ourselves.

But Jesus Christ was clear:

“Apart from me you can do nothing.” John 15:5

He did not say that apart from Him we can do less. He said we can do nothing.

And sooner or later, that “best version” of ourselves betrays us. We may be able to sustain it for a while. We can discipline ourselves, control ourselves, appear mature, and maintain certain behaviors. But something inside of us is still there.

Paul describes it:

“For what I am doing, I do not understand; for I am not practicing what I would like to do, but I am doing the very thing I hate.” Romans 7:15

And then he says:

“For I know that nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh.” Romans 7:18

Paul is not saying that everything about our humanity is useless, as though we were incapable of loving, working, creating, serving, or doing good things. He is saying that our flesh is not the source of the righteousness God requires.

James says something similar when he asks:

“What is the source of quarrels and conflicts among you? Is not the source your pleasures that wage war in your members?” James 4:1

The problem is not only outside of us. There is something within us that has been distorted by sin.

So we might say, “Well, maybe I am not perfect, but I will give God the best I have. My good works. My discipline. My effort. My righteousness.”

And then we come to Isaiah 64:6:

“All our righteous deeds are like a filthy garment.” Isaiah 64:6

That is powerful because Isaiah is not speaking only about our wickedness. He is speaking about our righteousness. About the very best we could present.

We can do good things. We can help, serve, sacrifice our time, give, love, and work. But none of those things can turn our righteousness into the righteousness of God.

That is why Paul says in Philippians 3:9 that he wanted to be found in Christ:

“Not having a righteousness of my own… but that which is through faith in Christ, the righteousness which comes from God.” Philippians 3:9

The gospel is not about improving my righteousness until it finally begins to look like God’s righteousness.

It is about recognizing that I need a righteousness that does not come from me.

That is why Christianity is not about Christ helping me become a better version of myself.

It is about something far more radical.

Paul says:

“I have been crucified with Christ; and it is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.” Galatians 2:20

And Romans 6:4 says that we were buried with Christ so that, just as He was raised from the dead, we too might walk in newness of life.

This is not simply the same person, only improved.

There is death.

There is a break with the old life.

There is new life.

And even when Paul speaks about everything he accomplished and how hard he worked, he says:

“I labored even more than all of them, yet not I, but the grace of God with me.” 1 Corinthians 15:10

I love that because Paul does not deny his effort. He worked. He obeyed. He fought. He persevered. But when he had to explain where all of it came from, he did not point to himself.

He pointed to grace.

And this reaches even into our desire to seek God.

If today there is within us a hunger to know Him, a desire to obey Him, sorrow over our sin, or a desire to become more like Christ, even that did not originate in us alone.

God has revealed Himself through creation, He has spoken to us through His Word, and ultimately He has revealed Himself to us in Christ.

And Philippians 2:13 says:

“For it is God who is at work in you, both to will and to work for His good pleasure.” Philippians 2:13

So I cannot even say, “Well, at least the desire is mine.”

Even the desire comes from Him.

That is why, in the end, Christianity cannot conclude with: “Look at what I managed to become.”

It must conclude with: “Look at what Christ has done.”

Because I do not need Christ to bring out the best in me.

I need Christ to be my life.

“It is no longer I who live, but Christ lives in me.” Galatians 2:20

Because the best version of me was nailed to the cross.

Author: Juan José Venegas
