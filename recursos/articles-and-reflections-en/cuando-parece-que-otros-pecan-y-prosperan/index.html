When it seems others sin and prosper

Base Text

"When I thought how to understand this, it was too painful for me, until I went into the sanctuary of God; then I understood their end." Psalm 73:16–17

Asaph nearly lost his footing while looking at the prosperity of the arrogant.

His problem was not imaginary: he saw real injustice. What changed was not immediately the situation, but his perspective before God. He understood that he was evaluating stories before reaching the end.

Sin can produce temporary advantages. Lying can open a door; exploitation can generate money; corruption can look profitable. The Bible never asks us to deny that.

It reminds us that momentary success and God's blessing are not synonyms.

Asaph ends by discovering that his true good is drawing near to God. That statement is deeper than "one day I will have more than they do."

If you obey only because you expect God to make you more prosperous than the unjust person, you are still using the same measuring stick.

Christ is our treasure. Do not sell a clean conscience for an advantage whose expiration date you cannot see.

Prayer

Lord, bring this truth of your Word into my life. Give me wisdom to obey you sincerely, a humble heart, and eyes fixed on Jesus Christ. Amen.
