When you failed again

Base Text

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness." 1 John 1:9

There are sins that bring particular shame because we had promised not to repeat them. Then a strange temptation appears: after sinning, to move away from the very One we most need to approach.

John writes to believers and rejects two deceptions. The first is saying that we have no sin. The second is living as if sin did not matter. The Christian response is neither denial nor indifference: it is confession.

To confess does not mean searching for a word that reduces our responsibility. It means agreeing with God about our sin. David did not call what he had done a "miscalculation." When he was confronted, he acknowledged, "I have sinned against the LORD."

But the gospel does not leave the confessing sinner crushed under endless condemnation. John immediately directs our eyes to Jesus Christ, our Advocate, and to him as the propitiation for our sins.

Grace does not make sin small. Precisely because sin is serious, the cross was necessary. And precisely because Christ died and rose again, failure does not have to become our final identity.

If you fell again, do not make peace with sin. But do not run away from God either. Confess, abandon what must be abandoned, seek help when necessary, and walk again in the light.

True repentance does not consist in punishing yourself until you feel you have paid. Christ does not need you to add a small personal cross to his. It consists in returning to God and walking again behind Jesus.

Prayer

Lord, I do not want to hide or justify my sin. I confess it before you. Thank you for the work of Christ; give me true repentance and strength to walk again in the light. Amen.
