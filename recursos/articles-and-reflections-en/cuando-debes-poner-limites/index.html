When you need to set boundaries

Base Text

"A prudent man foresees evil and hides himself, but the simple pass on and are punished." Proverbs 22:3

The Bible praises prudence. Loving your neighbor does not mean giving unlimited access to anyone.

Jesus loved everyone, but he did not entrust himself to everyone. Paul could forgive and at the same time warn about people who had caused harm.

Boundaries are not automatically a lack of forgiveness. They can protect responsibilities, family, conscience, and safety.

However, we can also call something a "boundary" when it is really an elegant form of revenge. That is why motive matters.

Are you seeking to prevent harm and act wisely, or to punish and control?

A healthy boundary is usually concrete: what behavior you will not accept and what you will do if it occurs. It does not require insults or threats.

In situations of abuse or danger, seeking protection and help does not contradict Christian love. Gentleness never means voluntarily offering yourself to evil.

Ask for wisdom to keep a heart free from hatred and, at the same time, eyes open.

Prayer

Lord, bring this truth of your Word into my life. Give me wisdom to obey you sincerely, a humble heart, and eyes fixed on Jesus Christ. Amen.
