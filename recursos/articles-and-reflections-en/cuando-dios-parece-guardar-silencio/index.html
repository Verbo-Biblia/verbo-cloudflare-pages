When God seems to remain silent

Base Text

"How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?" Psalm 13:1–2

There are prayers that seem to return without an answer. David knew that experience and did not disguise it with spirituality. Psalm 13 begins with a repeated question: "How long?" Biblical faith does not require us to pretend that waiting does not hurt.

But notice the movement of the psalm. David brings his anguish to God, asks him to look and answer, and ends by remembering his mercy. He did not resolve the silence by inventing an explanation. He turned toward the God who seemed silent.

That distinguishes biblical lament from despair without God. The believer can ask, weep, and acknowledge confusion without abandoning the One to whom he is speaking. Job asked questions. The Psalms are full of them. Even Jesus, on the cross, took the words of the psalmist upon his lips.

Sometimes we want an answer when God is calling us to remain. That does not mean every silence hides a secret explanation we can decipher. The Bible does not authorize us to invent reasons for why God allows each circumstance.

What we do know is his revealed character. In Christ we know that the silence of a moment does not mean the absence of love. The cross itself had hours in which the disciples could not understand what God was doing.

So do not turn the lack of an immediate answer into a verdict about God. Pray truthfully. Tell him what hurts. Ask for help. Remember what you already know of his mercy.

Faith does not always say, "I understand." Many times it says, "I do not understand, but I am still speaking to you."

Prayer

Lord, you know the questions that still have no answer. Teach me to seek you sincerely, to wait without inventing explanations, and to trust your character revealed in Christ. Amen.
