Spiritual pride

Base Text

"God, be merciful to me a sinner!... this man went down to his house justified rather than the other." Luke 18:13–14

The Pharisee in the parable prayed, fasted, and tithed. His problem was not doing religious things, but using them to build superiority.

His prayer became a comparison: "I am not like other men."

The tax collector could barely lift his eyes. Jesus said that this man went home justified.

Closeness to the things of God can produce a particular temptation: knowing enough truth to use it as a mirror in which we admire ourselves.

We can defend correct doctrine with a deeply proud heart.

Paul asks, "What do you have that you did not receive?" If everything is grace, boasting loses its oxygen.

Examine how you speak about believers who are less mature, about other people who sin, or about those who know less.

Truth must be defended, but we never need to despise someone in order to honor it.

The mature Christian is not the one who has accumulated reasons to feel superior, but the one who increasingly understands how deeply he depends on the mercy of God.

Prayer

Lord, bring this truth of your Word into my life. Give me wisdom to obey you sincerely, a humble heart, and eyes fixed on Jesus Christ. Amen.
