Anxiety about tomorrow

Base Text

"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about its own things." Matthew 6:34

Jesus did not speak these words to people living without problems. He spoke in a world where daily food, clothing, and economic security were real concerns. That is why his teaching is not an invitation to deny reality.

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus directs our eyes toward the Father. The birds do not manage storehouses and the lilies do not make garments, yet creation remains under God's care. The point is not that the believer will never face need, but that his life is not outside the Father's hands.

Worrying about tomorrow has a peculiar feature: it tries to suffer today problems that do not yet exist. Jesus brings the disciple back to the present day: "sufficient for the day is its own trouble."

That does not mean irresponsibility either. The Bible praises prudence, work, and preparation. Joseph stored food during years of abundance. Paul worked with his hands. Planning is not the same as living ruled by fear.

Jesus also sets a priority: seek first the kingdom of God and his righteousness. The Christian question is not merely, "How do I guarantee that nothing bad will happen?" but, "How do I live today under God's rule?"

Tomorrow will arrive with its own decisions. Today you have enough in front of you to obey.

Christ does not invite you to control the future. He invites you to follow him in the present, trusting the Father who already knows what you need.

Prayer

Father, free me from trying to control through worry what has not yet arrived. Give me wisdom to act today and confidence to leave tomorrow in your hands. Amen.
