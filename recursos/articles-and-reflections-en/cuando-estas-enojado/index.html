When you are angry

Base Text

"Be angry, and do not sin: do not let the sun go down on your wrath." Ephesians 4:26–27

Paul recognizes that anger exists without automatically identifying all anger as sin. Jesus was indignant at hardness of heart. But human anger is dangerous fuel. James warns that the wrath of man does not produce the righteousness of God.

We can begin angry about something real and end up enjoying hurting someone.

Ephesians commands us not to let anger give place to the devil. That means dealing with it, not feeding it.

Ask what truly happened, what you want to defend, and what part of your reaction comes from pride. Speak when appropriate, but avoid turning the conversation into punishment. Do not use silence, sarcasm, or volume to dominate.

And do not confuse forgiveness with avoiding necessary conversations. Christ calls us to speak truth and to do so as members of one another.

If your anger has already produced sin, acknowledge it without justifying it because of what the other person did.

Anger may point to a problem; it must never become your master.

Prayer

Lord, bring this truth of your Word into my life. Give me wisdom to obey you sincerely, a humble heart, and eyes fixed on Jesus Christ. Amen.
